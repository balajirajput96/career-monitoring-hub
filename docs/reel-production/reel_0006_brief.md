# Reel 0006 Brief — Sleep and Memory

## Working title

**पढ़ने के बाद नींद: क्या दिमाग सच में सीख को संभालता है?**

## Claim boundary

The reel may state that systematic reviews and meta-analytic evidence support a role for normal sleep in memory processes and that acute total sleep deprivation before or after learning is associated with poorer memory for newly learned material. It must not promise that sleeping after one study session guarantees retention, advise a medical treatment, claim that one sleep stage “stores” every memory, or use the cited deprivation effect sizes as a prediction for an individual.

## Evidence set

1. Weighall, A. & Kellar, I. (2023). *Sleep and memory consolidation in healthy, neurotypical children, and adults: a summary of systematic reviews and meta-analyses*. **Emerging Topics in Life Sciences**, 7(5), 513–524. https://doi.org/10.1042/ETLS20230110
2. Newbury, C. R., Crowley, R., Rastle, K., & Tamminen, J. (2021). *Sleep Deprivation and Memory: Meta-Analytic Reviews of Studies on Sleep Deprivation Before and After Learning*. **Psychological Bulletin**, 147(11), 1215–1240. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000348

## Distinct angle and visual direction

Use an original “library after learning” motion-graphics metaphor: a small warm memory card is filed into a nighttime, nonliteral neural archive while distracting daytime cards drift past outside. Use soft midnight indigo, muted coral, and moonlit mint; avoid medical imagery, brain scans presented as literal proof, or claims that sleep is a substitute for studying.
